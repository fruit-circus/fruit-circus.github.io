Zambia Q and A

~ So, these are some questions I got over the last few years. I wrote them down because I thought they were either funny or they revealed something about Zambian culture that might help you (my American audience) better understand Zambia and its relationship to the rest of the world.

The question-askers are all adults: mostly friends, neighbors, and co-teachers, but sometimes curious strangers. Each question is translated from kiiKaonde or paraphrased. ~



What do people farm in America?
Corn and soy beans, just like you! But we also grow a lot of wheat. It may surprise you to learn that fewer than one percent of people are farmers in America.

I thought everyone in America was rich, even the poor people.
The streets are not actually paved with gold. Actually, America even has people who are homeless, and the government does a pretty bad job of taking care of them.

So in America, there are no motorbikes?
There are.

Do you even wear sunscreen indoors when it’s hot?
No, just in direct sunlight.

I thought maybe you were Chinese.
No, I’m white.

I thought it was always cold in America?
The U.S. is big, so the climate is different in different parts of the country. In Florida, for example, it can actually be much hotter than Zambia, but in New York you can see several meters of snow.

How many provinces does America have?
About fifty.

When is the rainy season?
Actually it can rain any time of the year! And, in winter, when it's very cold in the sky, the rain can become snow.

Do big leaves also do photosynthesis? (I had used a small leaf in the example.)
Yes.

Are there people on other planets?
Maybe. But they probably don’t look like us. Probably they look like bacteria.

Do Muslims really eat babies?
No.

What’s the difference between insects and bacteria?
Bacteria are much smaller. About 10,000 times smaller. (In Kaonde, “kalongolo”, the word for bacteria, is derived from the word for insect, “kilongolo”. Ka, as a prefix, means “small”. An understandable conflation.)

Are you Indian?
No, I’m white.

Are there people on the moon?
Not right now.

Do you really eat nshima?
Yes! (Nshima: Imagine the texture of mashed potatoes and the taste of unflavored cornmeal.)

Do you like eating nshima?
It’s fine.

Do they eat nshima in America?
No, we do other things with our corn.

Do you wash your own dishes?
Yes?

Do you wash your own clothes?
Are you volunteering?

When you’re on a plane to China, it doesn’t really say “welcome to China”, does it?
It does actually. Maybe not in the plane, but in the airport.

Give me five kwacha.
What's your name?

Will you marry me and take me to America?
What's my name?

There are tall buildings in America, right? That means America is really big.
America does have a lot of cities, some of them are big and some of them are small.

Are there even witches in America? Do you believe in witchcraft / ghosts?
There are witches in America, but they’re different from the ones here. I don’t discount that it’s possible witchcraft and ghosts are real, but nothing I’ve seen suggests that it is. I wish I could say this belief was arrived at logically, but really it’s cultural. I would love to see something that shakes that belief. I think that would be really cool.

What causes lightning?
Great question. The world is made up of positive and negative energy, and sometimes when rain forms in the sky it creates an imbalance in that energy, which wants to resolve itself as quickly as possible. Essentially the sky becomes negative, and the ground positive, and so the two charges come crashing together, turning the air into plasma.

Are there tribes in America?
Yes, but not like there are here where everyone is part of a tribe. Before white people invaded, everyone belonged to a tribe.

Are the British and the Americans tribal cousins?
Basically, yes. But did you know that we also won our independence from Britain? It’s similar to how the Lozi and the Kaonde once fought wars but are now friendly.

So in America does everyone own a gun?
Yes, about one in three.

That's insane?
Yes, yes it is.

So, do you eat kapenta?
I’ve lived here for two years, do you think I’ve somehow gotten along without eating kapenta? (Kapenta is a tiny, salty, bony fish.)

Do you know Jack China?
You mean Jackie Chan.

Are Irish people Americans or do they have their own nation?
Uh. There are Irish Americans and there’s also an Ireland, but I don’t think you have to have Irish heritage to be an Irish citizen, so both.

In America is it a democracy or a dictatorship?
We like to think of ourselves as a “dictocracy”.

Do snakes really get that big?
Gladys, that is AI.

Oh yeah? Is this AI?
That’s a picture of your daughter’s graduation from nursing school.

Do dreams have meaning?
Depends on who you ask! Do ordinary thoughts have meaning? The only difference during sleep is that certain parts of the brain shut off, so your wild thought trains are uninterrupted by our relatively static, physical reality. But that's just what I think. I also think, for that reason, it's important to listen to your dreams, they can tell you how you really feel.

How did the first teacher become a teacher if there was no teacher before them?
I think probably all human beings have been teachers in informal capacities for hundreds of thousands of years, so the first formal teachers were just specialists who someone decided to pay to tutor their (likely royal) children. (Then I asked what they thought about the chicken and the egg and they said obviously the chicken came first because god created all the animals with words.) Aha, (I said,) but what if god created the chicken egg and told it to produce a chicken? (and they said Ah I suppose that’s possible.)

We didn’t really come from apes, did we?
No, we came from ape-like things. Apes are our tribal cousins.

You don’t really believe in evolution, do you?
One hundred years ago, people who spoke Kaonde said “chigayo”, “chipona”, and “chipongwe”. Now they say “kigayo” “kipona” and “kipongwe”. There’s no particular reason why they switched from “ch” to “k”, that’s just what started happening, and because they were in a different area than Bemba-speakers (who still use the "ch" sound), the change stuck, and Kaonde gradually became different from Bemba. Go back hundreds of years and eventually you’ll find a language that has features of both Bemba and Kaonde, a common ancestor that both languages emerged out of. Sadly we don’t know what this language sounded like, because words don’t leave fossils, but we can make pretty good guesses. With evolution we don’t have to guess, because many organisms do actually leave fossils.

So there aren’t public schools in America?
No, there are.

What language do they speak in America? Is it just English?
There’s no official national language. We have people who speak many different languages—Spanish, Mandarin, Arabic—but most people do know at least a little English.

Is this one of those Black Americans I’ve heard about?
That’s LeVar Burton.

Do you have a device that can detect gemstones and can I borrow it?
Give me five kwacha.
